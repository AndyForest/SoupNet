## What the field showed since slice 5

1. **Drafts are the most urgent thing in the corpus,** because a draft needs an action by design and a verified recipe does not. The sweep reads drafts every tick and routes each to the session that can settle it; it reads new recipes only hourly (recipe `8381753a`, personal book). 40 drafts were open on 2026-10-01, 56 on 10-21, 62 on 10-24, across three books; the oldest is from 2026-09-28.
2. **Agents refused to ratify drafts their context settled.** An orchestrator decided it would verify only drafts the person had confirmed in their own words, and a sub-agent justified both of its drafts with "Draft because [the person] is not in this session". The operator had already ruled the opposite on 2026-09-27 (recipe `200ed4fe`: a person's own agent ratifies "in the exact same way that it can make recipe checks autonomously if it's confident in the same ways") and called the refusal "over cautious ... it points towards an ossified assumption somewhere in your context". The assumption traced to the tool copy, which is the only description of drafts an agent meets without the briefing: `verify_draft` says "once they confirm it", the `draft` parameter says "the person can't be asked about now", and the divergent-checks section says "wait for the user".
3. **No agent path closes a draft the person rejected.** In one piece of work, 18 drafts were settled with the person: 8 published, 9 answered "no" or delegated, 1 open. The 9 could only get a feedback row (disposition `corrected`) and the person's actual position logged as new recipes; each stayed unverified, so every sweep found it again. The person closes them one click at a time in the queue. [pr-review-helpers.md](pr-review-helpers.md) §6 already names the smallest addition: one outcome value on the existing verify operation.
4. **The defer pattern was refined.** Slice 6 was specified from recipe `ea930c3d` (both options as linked drafts). On 2026-10-01 the operator extended it (recipe `7942b8c0`): when every option is genuinely pursued, "recipe check all the options (not even as drafts, since we are genuinely adopting all the options)", evaluate, check the chosen path with the outcome as evidence, and leave feedback rows on the earlier recipes. Both forms are real; the second needs no resolution operation at all.
5. **Orchestration is live, not deposit-then-review** (recipe `97f7c34e`). Sub-agents decide as they go and surface only high impact × uncertainty checks; the orchestrator polls its drafts during the run and corrects mid-run. Today the only poll is `is:draft after:<run start>`, which also picks up other sessions' drafts. An `intent:` qualifier is what makes the poll exact.
6. **An unattended reader cannot walk a list.** `is:draft` returns clustered exemplars (62 drafts came back as 3 exemplars on 2026-10-24); nothing bounds a search by creation time; and a book's Members line is omitted for a solo book, so a sweep cannot tell "nobody joined yet" from "the line is hidden", which is what releases drafts queued for a colleague who has not accepted an invite. The session-walk workaround (same `session_id`, search again, compare collected ids with `totalResults`) works, costs a full response of recipe text per step, and small-model readers stop short (9 of 18, 18 of 57 on different days). The sweep made about 670 reads in three weeks.
7. **Agents read recipes as rules.** Four cases in three weeks, each corrected by the operator in their own words: a matching recipe treated as settling a new instance ("already recorded"); a question put to the person that the files answered; an older recipe called "the rule" that a newer judgment "narrowed"; a rule proposed for another session without a check. "You're drifting back to soupnet being 'rules' instead of a log of context dependent taste / judgement calls." The echo warning ("a bare restatement dedupes against its original") was being stretched into "a similar recipe exists, so skip".
8. **Three more briefing gaps,** each with a field case and proposed text: claims drifting hyper-specific (one word, "granular", doing two jobs: count and specificity); no role pattern for consumption-side taste (what the person receives rather than does); and no named trigger for instruction-versus-judgment tension.

## Slice 6, revised and split: resolution first, then the intent link, then sets

The design's own sentence holds: "The intent is the set; no new 'option set' concept is needed." What changes is that a set may hold ordinary recipes as well as drafts, that the one resolution operation also closes rejected and superseded drafts, and that the three parts ship as three PRs in the order the field needs them: 6c, then 6a, then 6b with 6d.

### 6a. The recipe-to-intent link

- `traces.intent_id`, nullable, written at deposit when the check carries an `int_…` id, as the feedback path already does (`check_feedback.intent_id`). Nothing else writes it. Export carries it; import restores it when the intent is the importer's, else drops it. This closes gap 2 of [../architecture/agent-context-seams.md](../architecture/agent-context-seams.md).
- A qualifier `intent:<id>` in the search grammar, for people and agents, selecting any recipe the viewer may read. With `is:draft` it reads "unresolved drafts about me under this intent", which is the orchestrator's exact poll (field item 5) and the queue's grouping (DT-OPT-01). The qualifier is a selection, not a score, so it is built outside the ranking files as the rating filters were (ruling 23).
- The queue lists drafts sharing an intent together, under the intent's story as a heading, in triage order within the group.

### 6b. What a set is, and two shapes of resolution

A set is the recipes under one intent whose story names the choice. Two shapes, both first-class:

- **Options as drafts** (recipe `ea930c3d`): the agent cannot yet stand behind either option. Resolution verifies the winner and marks the siblings not chosen, in one call, as ruling 17 says. The winner's evidence cites the measurement; a not-chosen draft stays hidden like any draft (DT-OPT-03, ruling 18 accepted).
- **Options as ordinary recipes** (recipe `7942b8c0`): every option is genuinely pursued, so each claim is true when checked. No resolution operation: the agent checks the chosen path with the outcome as evidence, under the same intent, and leaves a feedback row on each earlier option (`proceeded` on the one kept, `corrected` or `deferred` on the others). The intent link is what joins them for a later reader.

No "not chosen" state for published recipes. The recipes were true when deposited; the outcome lives in the final check and the feedback join, which the detail page already shows. Revisit if the field asks for a visible marker on the options that lost. (Four weeks in, it has not: the field has used the ordinary-recipe shape and the drafts shape has not been run.)

### 6c. One outcome parameter on the verify operation (ship first)

`verify_draft` and `POST /recipes/:id/verify` gain `outcome: verified | rejected | not_chosen`, default `verified`, so the operation is "resolve a draft" with the same name. This closes the agent-reject gap (field item 3, [pr-review-helpers.md](pr-review-helpers.md) §5 and §6) and keeps one tool with one contract (recipe `6ae9a299`) instead of a second tool paying the roster cap. The operator chose this shape on 2026-10-18 (recipe `d21d3894`); the field has been citing that recipe as the fix it waits on since.

- Authority is unchanged: the subject's keys, `keyMayVerifyDrafts` (headless keys cannot; ruling 13, S5-M2's doc comment already names this operation).
- The evidence rule is unchanged and structural (ruling 14): a new entry with a quote and a citation that duplicates none of the draft's evidence. For a rejection the quote is the person's answer, or the verified recipe that covers the same call, cited by id. Open question 46 asks whether context-only evidence is enough for rejection; the recommendation is yes, by the same reasoning as `200ed4fe`: the person's own agent holds the context and can reach them, and a rejection hides a hypothesis where a verification publishes one. The superseded case (a later verified recipe covers the call; thirteen of the field's drafts) is `rejected` with that recipe cited; no fourth outcome.
- Resolving a set is `outcome: verified` on the winner with `resolve_siblings: not_chosen` (or the siblings named by id), one call, one transaction, one audit row per draft in the existing `recipe.draft_*` shapes. This part lands with 6b; the single-draft outcomes land first.
- The queue's one-click reject and the reaction route are untouched.
- In the same PR, since it is the same operation and the field hits it: parse `verify_draft` evidence the way `check_recipe` does (one entry is interpretation, quote, citation; blank lines between entries), and have the refusal say how many entries the text split into.

### 6d. Drop the server-side rubric distinction (ruling 17, DT-OPT-04)

Ruling 17 made agent resolution depend on citing "a rubric the person gave", because "the server can't tell a rubric the person gave from one the agent wrote". That is the same distinction the operator declined for verification: the person's own agent resolves under its confidence rule, and the server enforces only that the evidence is new. Keeping a stricter rule for sets than for single drafts would reproduce field item 2. Recommendation: DT-OPT-04 becomes "An agent resolves a set under the same confidence rule as a single draft; a set it cannot settle stays in the queue with the measurements in its evidence", and "state the rubric before measuring" becomes briefing guidance in slice 7, where it does its work. One field flag bears on this: of five drafts one session superseded, four were superseded by recipes an agent had verified, not the person; the audit trail (`draft_resolved_by_key_id`, and the superseding id in the evidence) is what lets the person see that later.

### 6e. Budget

Remote `tools/list` is at 16,986 of 17,000 bytes. One new parameter on `verify_draft`, one on `check_recipe` (none: `intent` already exists), and one grammar term in the `search_recipes` description do not fit. A dated raise, sized to the measured addition, as slice 2 did (recipe `6ae9a299`, recipe `8dd573b4`). The shared-description cap (6,051 of 6,080) takes the same treatment.

### 6f. Rubric sketch

Rows the slice 6 rubric should carry, for the rubric author to make pass/fail:

- `outcome` defaults to `verified`; `rejected` and `not_chosen` write the same columns the queue writes, the same audit shapes, and no reaction row; the depositor of an on-behalf draft gets the honest refusal; everyone else the uniform 404 (S4-R2, S3-A3 re-run with the new outcomes).
- A rejection whose only new evidence is a verified recipe cited by id passes the structural rule; one that re-quotes the draft's own evidence is refused with the existing message.
- `verify_draft` evidence with blank lines inside one entry is accepted as one entry; the same text is accepted by both the MCP tool and the REST twin.
- Headless keys get `key_cannot_verify` for every outcome.
- `intent_id` written only at deposit, only from a resolved `int_` id; never by import of a foreign intent; the static seam test lists the one writer.
- `intent:` composes with every existing qualifier; an unknown or unreadable intent id yields the empty set with the uniform note, so the qualifier is not an existence oracle for other people's intents.
- The queue groups by intent with the story as heading; ungrouped drafts keep today's order.
- Resolving a set is atomic: 18 concurrent resolutions of one set yield one winner and one audit row per draft (the S3-A2 shape).
- Ranking isolation unchanged; `intent` joins the rating terms in the guard's forbidden list for the five ranking files.
- Byte counts before and after, with the raise declared.

## Slice 7, revised: briefing copy

### 7a. Reframe when to draft

The design's section opens "Drafting exists for moments when the human can't be asked", and the `@unreleased` scenario in `checking-behavior.feature` says "An agent drafts only when it cannot ask". The field read that literally (item 2), and then read it the other way too (drafts parked for an absent person who was in fact in the session). Proposed frame, which the operator chose on 2026-10-18 (recipe `5d76a576`): **a draft is a hypothesis the agent cannot yet stand behind from the context it holds.** The person being unreachable is one reason a call stays unsettled; the person being reachable is not by itself a reason to interrupt them, since the briefing already says agents settle what their context settles. The section then carries the order of moves the field converged on, and four points that are each already a ruling:

- At a call you cannot stand behind from what you hold: if you will act on it, check it as an ordinary recipe and own it; if it is the person's to make and they are reachable, ask them in plain words with your recommendation and its reasoning; draft only when neither applies, say in the first evidence entry what would settle it, and return to it when that lands. A draft is not a note for the person's to-do list.
- The person's own agents ratify drafts under the same confidence rule as an ordinary check, with the settling context as evidence; ask the person only when you judge it necessary or useful (`200ed4fe`).
- Orchestrating sub-agents is live: sub-agents decide as they go and surface only high impact × uncertainty checks without blocking; poll your drafts by intent during the run; correct mid-run; ratify what you can settle; raise the rest; after the run, resolve your sub-agents' drafts or list them with a recommendation (`97f7c34e`).
- A call worth deferring: when every option will genuinely be pursued, check each as an ordinary recipe; when you cannot stand behind any, draft them under one intent with the rubric stated before measuring; either way, check the chosen path with the outcome as evidence and leave feedback on the rest (`7942b8c0`, `ea930c3d`).

The scenario is edited to match: given the person is reachable, the agent settles from context, asks, or presents options; given it cannot settle the call, it drafts and says what would settle it.

### 7b. Tool copy that contradicts the rulings

Slice 2 and 5 copy, corrected under the regression rule in the same declaration:

- `verify_draft`: "Publish your user's draft recipe once they confirm it" becomes "Publish, reject, or mark not chosen a draft about your user once you or they can stand behind the answer: new evidence quoting their words, or the context that settles it. Evidence that adds nothing new is refused." (Also carries slice 6's outcomes.)
- `draft` parameter: "the person can't be asked about now" becomes "a high-impact, uncertain call you cannot settle yet from the context you hold".
- The draft deposit notice: "To publish it, ask the person and call verify_draft" becomes "Resolve it with verify_draft when you or another agent acting for the person can settle it, or hand them the queue link."
- `HEADLESS_KEY_SECTION`: "say why your user couldn't be asked and what would settle it" becomes "say what would settle it".
- Divergent recipe checks: one sentence carving out the pursue-every-option case from "wait for the choice, then check only the chosen one".
- The queue page's intro and the dashboard's drafts card say the agents parked these "because they could not ask you"; same correction, web side, under the same declaration.

### 7c. The planned slice 7 content holds

The "checkable by someone who wasn't there" phrase in Truthfulness; the rating guidance (the scenario at `checking-behavior.feature` line 57); the headless profile, now reading "how to draft well, what would settle each draft, the ratings, and building both" with the reframed definition.

### 7d. General briefing additions from the field

Each is a few lines, has a field case, and gets its own `.feature` scenario under the regression rule. Items 1 to 4 were proposed on 10-18; 5 to 8 come from the second read. Recommended order by the harm their absence has caused:

1. **A returned recipe is a call someone made in their context, not a rule** (field item 7). Three bullets in the results-interpretation section: weigh it against your situation and say how it applies ("in their context they chose X"); recurrence is not restatement, so a familiar judgment in a new context is logged with that context as evidence; write claims as choices ("I chose", "I prefer"), one judgment per recipe, no "never" or "must". Product companion, the operator's call: the result header "N similar recipe(s) found" could read "N past calls in similar contexts".
2. **Two more moments in "When to check"**, each as an example and a goal, never a step owed (the operator's form): *extending something that already has a default* (who is let through when no rule matches; the field's one-row refusal list that became a launch policy; recipe `72f788bf`), and *writing for a particular reader in a particular place* (the field's twenty-five corrections in three weeks, all facts right and the first draft unusable, with the correction already in the corpus in most cases and no check made before writing in any).
3. **Granularity and generality are two axes** (field item 8): one paragraph appended to Authoring for retrieval. One judgment per recipe is the count axis; how much of this case sits inside the claim is the specificity axis; state the claim at the most general level that is still true and put the specifics in the evidence, where they are still read and still checkable. The detail moves, it is not deleted.
4. **Recipient roles for consumption-side taste**: a fourth role pattern. When the judgment is about what the person receives (a document, a report, a dashboard), the recipient role is the invariant one; name the kind of thing received, keep the goal clause separate, and use "prefer" or "chose", not "want". The test: does it stay true when the same person authors the next one rather than reads it. The field measured the cost of a merged role: three otherwise identical recipes whose roles differed returned different clusters, and the merged role's cluster belonged to neither.
5. **Tension between instructions and judgment is a check trigger**: a bullet in When to check. Check the call you genuinely think is right, then proceed; the judgment is logged either way and the id is where later redirection attaches.
6. **Your own open draft is not evidence.** In Search vs check or the results section: a neighbour that is your own unverified draft, or one deposited today under your intent, is your assumption returning in the person's voice; weigh it as such. (Product companion in slice 8: set own open drafts apart in check results.)
7. **A probe of the corpus is a search, not a check.** Two role probes made with `check_recipe` took exemplar slots that compete with the original. One line in Search vs check.
8. **Thin results mean think harder, not stretch.** The operator's words (recipe `b7e90f25`): "When results in your specific context are thin, I expect you to be more thoughtful, and log more recipe checks in general." One line beside the empty-result message.

## Slice 8, "unattended readers": the read paths and tool shapes a fleet needs

The first three are the 10-18 items; the rest are the tool-shape gaps the field logged with evidence. None adds a noun. They decide whether the drafts feature is usable without a person polling the queue, and they cost every reader on every tick today.

1. **A flat, paged list for qualifier-only searches.** With no semantic text there is nothing to cluster on. Return id, dates, book, ratings, state, and the claim's first line, newest first (triage order for `is:draft`), with a `page` parameter. The queue service already does this for people; this gives the same listing to the key that deposited the drafts. Done well, it retires the session-walk workaround from every reader brief in the field.
2. **"Changed since"** (a draft verified or rejected, feedback added, a recipe moved), with creation time as the simpler fallback. A sweep asks what arrived or moved since it last looked, which is not the judgment date that `after:` filters.
3. **Membership an agent can act on:** always print a Members line on shared-capable books ("Members (1): you"), and a "Pending invites (n)" line for books the caller owns.
4. **`get_recipes` accepts an array of ids** as well as the comma-separated string (the parameter name invites the array; three sessions sent one).
5. **Validation errors name every failing field at once and the enumerations live in the parameter descriptions** (`log_feedback`, `verify_draft`): a row should land on the first retry, not the fifth. Unknown parameter names are refused, not ignored.
6. **An `outcome` feedback row joins on `intent_id` alone**, and `get_briefing` returns an id a feedback row can carry, so the surface being tuned can be scored.
7. **The exclude-own message offers the `author:me` form of the same query** as its first line when every match was hidden by the default (the 2026-09-19 field report's finding 6, still hit).
8. **Own open drafts set apart in check results:** a separate line after the exemplars ("your open drafts near this call"), never an exemplar slot, so an agent's morning assumption does not come back as its evening evidence.
9. **A related item that outscores every exemplar is promoted** to an exemplar slot, or shown in full. Display-layer only; the ranking guard decides whether it touches a guarded file, and if it does the item goes to ranking research instead.

Smaller, for the backlog: the drill-down hint says "re-check with this recipe text", which sends a reader to the write path (it should say "search with this text"); a response can print two similarity figures for one pair without saying which is which; a context line beside each exemplar's claim (role context and first evidence source) would keep agents from quoting the claim alone.

## A proposed slice 9, "read paths for reports": intents and feedback

The operator's direction on 2026-10-24: a read path for intents and feedback would be very useful for creating reports. Today neither is readable by an agent, and intents are not readable by anyone. The declared-intent design (Phase C) already said "expose the graph before designing features on it"; this is that exposure.

- **`GET /intents`** (Bearer key): the caller's user's intents, newest first, paged: id, story, agent id, key label, created, last used; counts of recipes shown to it, recipes deposited under it (after 6a), and feedback rows carrying it. **`GET /intents/:id`**: the same plus the lineage, the shown ids, the deposits, and the feedback rows, in time order. Own intents only; a foreign id is the uniform not-found.
- **`GET /feedback`** as a listing (Bearer key), beside the existing write form: filters `intent_id`, `trace_id`, `search_id`, `agent_id`, `since`, `recipe_book`; paged; each row as the per-recipe route already shapes it. Scope is the open question 54: the caller's user's rows (a report on your own fleet, the case the operator named), or every row on a recipe the caller can read (what the web page shows the person).
- **Web:** render the intent id on feedback rows on the detail page (the route already returns it) and give an intent its own page, reachable from a feedback row and from the queue's intent heading (6a).
- **Not an MCP tool, at first.** Reports are made by scripts and by agents outside a session; REST with the key serves both and costs nothing on the tool roster. Reopen as a tool if in-session agents need their own intent's lineage at check time; the `intent:` qualifier (6a) covers the recipe half of that already.
- Exports: feedback rows and intents join the corpus export so a self-hoster's report is complete.

## Considered and set aside

Each with the reason, and what would reopen it.

- **A separate `reject_draft` tool.** Set aside for one outcome parameter on the verify operation: one contract, no roster cost beyond the parameter, and the queue already treats confirm, reject, and not chosen as one resolution with three values. Reopen if the outcomes diverge in authority or evidence rules.
- **A fourth outcome, `superseded`.** Thirteen of the field's drafts are this case. Set aside because it is a rejection with a particular kind of evidence (the covering recipe, cited by id), and the evidence is where the link belongs; a fourth value would need its own queue affordance and audit shape. Reopen if readers of a rejected draft cannot find the covering recipe from its page.
- **A feedback disposition for rejection** (what the field used as a workaround, twice more this week). Feedback records calibration about a check; it never changes a draft's state, and sweeps cannot read it to learn a draft was dealt with. Reopen never; the workaround is the evidence against it.
- **An "option set" entity** (a table, an id, a membership list). The intent already is the set; a second grouping would need its own authz, export, and deletion rules. Reopen if sets need to span intents, which nothing in the field suggests.
- **Server-side provenance of the rubric** (ruling 17 as written). The server cannot tell the person's words from the agent's; the confidence rule the operator chose for verification already governs. Reopen if agent resolutions of sets prove wrong at a rate the person notices; the audit trail is what would show it, and slice 9 is what would let anyone count it.
- **Detecting reachability server-side** (for example, "the person has a live session"). Not knowable and not the right test; confidence is. Not reopened.
- **A "not chosen" state on published recipes** for the pursue-every-option shape. The recipes were true when checked; feedback rows and the final check carry the outcome. Reopen if readers of a losing option cannot find the outcome from its detail page.
- **Deleting rejected drafts.** The field asked whether a rejected draft is a record of what the person does not hold or clutter. Slice 2 keeps them readable by id to the two parties and out of every result set, which answers both; a verified recipe that covers the same call (cited in the rejection's evidence) is the durable record. Reopen with the import question the slice 4 verifier raised (rejected drafts in an export re-import as fresh drafts).
- **Giving agents the web queue endpoint.** It is tied to the person's login by design; the flat list in slice 8 reaches the same service through the agent surface.
- **Raising the exemplar count** instead of a flat list. It moves the ceiling and still hides members; a sweep needs every id.
- **Pushing drafts to the orchestrator** (a callback or stream). Polling by intent is enough for the live workflow and needs nothing new on the server; sub-agents can also pass ids in their reports. Reopen if poll cost shows up in a sweep's budget.
- **MCP tools for intents and feedback.** The roster is at its cap and reports are made outside sessions; REST first (slice 9). Reopen if agents need the lineage in-session.
- **Telling a shared book's owner whether a member's agent checks.** The first thing such an owner wants to know, and a real gap; set aside here because it is a privacy design (checks and feedback are per user) that organization accounts should settle, not a drafts slice.
- **An inbound check page** (a check URL without a key that a signed-in person previews and submits). A real proposal from the same field period, but a separate feature with its own auth surface; it does not touch drafts and is not folded in here.
- **Pulling the organization-domain case into slice 6.** On behalf of someone who has not joined the book stays with organization accounts; the field's queue files per colleague are the workaround and were designed as one.
- **The "after meaningful work" check trigger.** The field found closing checks mostly retrieve the session's own opening check. A removal, so left alone until measured; slice 9 is what would measure it.
